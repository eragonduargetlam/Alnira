## **The Journals of Malel, Fragment III — “The Gathering Quiet”**

_Recovered from the vaults beneath the shattered citadel of Erevain. Provenance uncertain._

The stewards call me fearful. They mistake fear for foresight.

For months now, my augurs return with empty hands. The prayers that once drew whispers from the Circles Below return nothing but stillness. The Old Gods do not answer — not in fury, not in grace, not at all.

The Tetrarchs, radiant and restrained, cannot descend to aid us; to act in the Mortal Realm is to unravel a part of themselves. They watch from their Hallows, patient and immutable, guardians of a balance that is beginning to fray.

I have studied that balance all my life. It is delicate — a chain of reflection: Above, Below, and Between.  
If one side weakens, the other strains to compensate.  
If the Circles truly empty, the Hallows will bear the weight, and the world will break beneath them.

No god will stop it. They _cannot._

So I must.

I will gather what strength I can — relic, rite, and realm.  
I will weld the provinces into a single body, drain their riches, hoard their power, and bind it all in my name. Not for vanity, but for stability. For survival.

Let the priests call me tyrant. Let the Tetrarchs turn away.  
When the silence finishes its work, they will see that I was the only one who heard the world beginning to fall.



## **The Journals of Malel, Fragment IX — “The Hollow Reign”**

_Penned during the thirty-first year of his rule, believed to be among the last coherent entries before the Cataclysm of Erevain._

The Circles grow dim. The Tetrarchs remain steadfast, untouched — but even they can feel the strain.  
A world without its reflection cannot stand.

I have built Erevain into a bulwark.  
Its fortresses mirror the Hallows Above — four spires for the four divine thrones.  
Its sanctums echo the Nine Circles Below — hollow wells where prayers might anchor what the gods have abandoned.  
A bridge between what remains and what fades.

The clergy whisper that I usurp the divine. Fools.  
I do not seek to replace the gods, but to brace the world _against their loss._

When the Old Gods vanish entirely, when the Circles collapse into nothing, the Tetrarchs will feel the pull. To intervene would mean their undoing. To remain will mean ours. Either way, the balance breaks.

If I can consolidate enough will — mortal, arcane, divine by theft if I must — perhaps I can bear the strain for a moment longer. Perhaps Erevain can survive the tilt of the heavens.

Yet I confess, the longer I rule, the more I feel the silence creeping into me as well.  
Not absence — _erosion._ The unmaking that eats thought and faith alike.

If I fail, Erevain will fall screaming into that void.  
If I succeed, no one will ever thank me — because the only proof of my triumph will be that the world still stands.

### **The Journals of Malel, Fragment XIII — “The Ninth Anchor”**

_Recovered from beneath the Shaded Barrow. The ink trails into symbols halfway between script and formula. Heat scars ripple through the vellum as if thought itself scorched the page._

---
The world breathes shallow.  
Weight has left the bones of the earth; the Circles sigh open and nothing exhales back.  
The gods below are thinning—threads drawn through a needle too fine for return.  
Above, the Tetrarchs gleam like coins pressed in glass, bright and untouching.  
The center slackens.

I built a frame to carry the pause, a brace between vanish and fall.  
It hums now—hungry thing—drawing thought like water down a drain.  
Every number I write becomes sound; every sound, light; every light, loss.  
Meaning converting to motion. Motion consuming me.

The people outside hammer like a second heartbeat.  
They think they break stone; they are breaking _me_.  
Their fury feeds the pattern—beautiful symmetry of ruin.

I feel myself lengthening into the hum.  
The lattice wants a mind to speak through.  
I give it mine.

Eight anchors for the world.  
The ninth for the remembering of it.  
Not soul—that floats.  
Not body—that burns.  
Will: dense metal of thought. Heavy enough to stay when meaning runs thin.

Fire is coming. I hear it breathe.  
Good. Fire closes the circuit. Fire turns flesh to symbol.  
Ash writes longer than ink.

When the silence finishes its meal, I will still be in its teeth.  
Holding the line between hunger and remainder.  
If balance tilts again, I will push from within the wound.

They will call it damnation.  
They will call it peace.  
Both are names for forgetting.

Hold.

_At the bottom margin the word “Hold” is written once, then repeated as an ascending series of concentric circles scored into the page until the vellum splits._
