Opening of [[Beneath the Festival Moon (completed 8.31.25)]]

A quinquennial event performed at [[Lydborough]] both in celebration and as superstition to keep the evil king [[Malel]] from returning. 

## Primary attractions:
Afternoon Bazaar
		A gathering in the central square attended by many outsider peddlers, an opportunity to share the news from afar, flaunt wealth, and gain needed replacements for tools at festival prices
			Merchants:
				[[Bannick Halder]]
				[[Yhessa Dunwheel]]
			Villagers:
				[[Nym Garrel]]
				[[Old Breff]]
			Herbalist:
				[[Thenya Mossroot]]
	Dawn's End Feast
		Clearing of the bazaar and setting down of tables. Sponsored feast by the manor lord, presided over by the effigy of [[Malel]]. Tradition is to toast each course to the 'kings health'. A friendly competition among musicians and storytellers - winner lights the flame
			Competitors:
				[[Nella Raye]]
				[[Maeryn the Weaver]]
				[[Trant Dovven]]
	The Burning
		Winner of the competition is provided the ceremonial torch from the [[Flamekeeper]], [[Argenna Hollowbind]]. The phrase shouted aloud without hesitation "By flame and cheer, we end his reign, this wicked king shall not rise again. Let sparks fly high, let laughter ring, Tonight we burn the shadowed king"
		Followed by the [chant] while the flames catch
			Ash to ash, his throne undone,
			Malel falls with the dying sun.
			Flame defend and frost beware
			He shall not rise from smoke or air!
Interrupt on second cycle of chant with:
#IncitingIncident Just as the effigy’s flames reach their peak, a sudden wind knifes through the crowd—**sharp, unnatural, and ice-cold**. The fire gutters, then dies in a burst of ash, leaving only embers swirling in the moonlight.

A tense silence follows—broken by a scream. Then another.

From the edge of the village, torches bob and vanish between buildings. **Figures dart through the shadows**, hunched and fast, dragging sacks and bundles—**goblins**, you realize, and they're already vanishing into the treeline, the stolen goods clutched in clawed hands.

Panic ripples through the festival as villagers cry out and guards scramble. The trail is fresh—but fading fast.
