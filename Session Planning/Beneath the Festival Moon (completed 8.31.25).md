# Setting
Act 1 - Status Quo
	Opens on the feast of [[Flamebound Exaltation]] prior to the contest. Allow time for roleplay during feast, possibility for jovial contests perhaps even for one of the PCs to compete.

#IncitingIncident Icy cold wind blows up during second refrain of [[chant]] extinguishing the flames. During the confusion, shrieks cry out at the periphery: a small raiding band of [[Goblin Raiders]] are seen making off with bundles containing food, goods and warm clothing

Act 2 - Rising action
	[[Tracking the Goblins]] back to the [[Shaded Barrow]], specifically the [[- Burial Chamber of Malel]]
#Climax Battle with the [[Ice Troll Ritual Chamber]]

Act 3 - Falling action
	Treasure found after the Ice Troll falls

Closing:
	The return:
			The players return as unexpected heroes. The villagers celebrate the return of their goods, and presumed safety from further goblin raids. Their joy is dimmed, however when the players tell their story: Malel's barrow was disturbed, and his sarcophagus lies empty.
			The winds still carry a chill, out of season and out of place.
	Rewards:
			If the goods are returned, one of the merchants asks meekly about a bolt of red cloth that seemed to be among what the goblins took. If confirmed by the PCs to have recovered it, he is grateful stating it was a dear cost to procure and he had hoped to fetch a great price at court further east [[Velcairn]]. If given back, he laments the state of the [[Rosemere Fenlace]] - streaks of altar soot and frost. He offers instead to sell it to the PCs at a great discount, both in recognition for their deed and because he has no faith he can fetch a good price from it now. If the PCs evaluate the cloth after agreeing to the price, the soot wipes away easily though the silk remains nearly ice cold regardless of surrounding temperature. Clearly this merchant didn't know the half of what he was about to sell.
			[[Bone dice cup carved with crescent marks]] The markings mean nothing to the villagers. An elder simply shakes their head and says, "Not ours, nor ever was. Best left to dust" an old herbalist is heard to say "Best not to roll bones taken from the cold. Bad luck in every cast."
			[[Sealed Clay Phial of Blackened Honey]] The glass glows faintly when held near flame. No one knows what it is and most would rather not. "Too sweet," says one. "Too sweet for anything good." The same herbalist if asked raises a wicker charm between her and the party when shown, "That ain't swamp sweet," she says. "That's *elsewhere*"
	The Festival
		The closing ceremony restarts as the sun rises. The villagers gather in silence. No effigy but a simple pyre erected in the square, layered with dry cedar and wrapped in herb-smoked cloth. No speech is given. No name is uttered. The elderly Flamekeeper steps forward and lowers a torch into the wood.
			"For what has passed," she says, 'and for what may never rise again'
		The fire crackles to life, a small defiance in the chill morning. Around it, the villagers watch quietly, arms folded against the cold, a thin snowfall drifting down to speck the ash-darkened stones.
		As the snow falls, villagers begin to speak but only in hushed tones. Slowly they realize the party is still there and their eyes soften. Nods are given. Shoulders clasped. Bread pressed into palms. A shawl, freshly stitched is draped over one of the party members. They don't say it directly--but the message is clear:
			You kept the fire burning. We saw what passed in the dark and now we know what to watch for.
		A few brave children hand rosemary sprigs to the party instead of casting them into the pyre - tokens of thanks and, perhaps, protection. One of the children calls them "[[Ash-Wardens]]", though the name is quickly hushed by his mother.
		As the pyre smolders, its soot is drawn across doorways and the snowfall continues, dusting rooftops in unfamiliar white. The villagers retreat slowly this time --Not from indifference, but from recognition: something has changed.
		And in that final glance from the Flamekeeper, quiet and knowing, there is respect, but also a warning:
			"The wind remembers things it once forgot. Walk safely."
