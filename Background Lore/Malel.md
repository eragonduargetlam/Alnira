A tyrant so hated his subjects rose in bloody rebellion, waging a brutal five-year civil war to bring him down. When they finally did, they burned him and buried what remained deep beneath the [[Shaded Barrow]], sealing it with powerful wards. But his subjects in [[Lydborough]] didn't forget him. Even now, their descendants burn his image in effigy during the [[Flamebound Exaltation]]; not just for celebration. There is a fear that neglecting the rite like the rest of Old [[Erevain]] might invite his return.

The truth in his reign:
	Once a brilliant and inquisitive scholar, Malel uncovered a collection of indecipherable texts long abandoned by academia. Branded the ravings of a madman, these writings had been dismissed due to their impenetrable script --an ancient, forbidden language once spoken only by the [[Faint Divinities]].
	Malel's obsession with the texts led him to uncover a terrible truth: they were transcripts of oral traditions dating back to before [[Dawn Concord]]. He delved deeper, making contact with the hidden [[Cults of the Old Gods]], hoping they could teach him the rituals needed to contact [[The Circles Below]].
	Most rituals failed. The near Circles had grown silent.
	Increasingly desperate and disturbed by the cryptic messages within the texts, Malel turned to forbidden magic and acts of grave evil: blood rites, sacrifices and dark conjurations. Finally, he succeeded in contact on of the so-called demons, a [[Faint Divinities]]. 
	The entity spoke only a single phrase before vanishing:
		"A great silence is coming"
	Convinced that a [[Divine Vacuum]] was imminent --that gods would vanish or fall inert--Malel abandoned his scholarly life and began a relentless campaign to seize magical and political power. He became a tyrant, feared and hated, willing to do anything to prepare the world for what he believed was an oncoming collapse of divine order.
	Though his people eventually overthrew him and sealed his barrow, Malel's legacy --and the mystery of the silence he foresaw-- endures.

A bardic retelling, sterilized:
	Hear now the tale of Malel the Grim,
	Whose hunger for power o'ershadowed him.
	A scholar once, with eyes too wide,
	He cracked the gate where demons hide.
	He chased old whispers, deep and foul,
	And wore the crown with a curs-ed scowl.
	His magic bled the land and sky,
	To climb where only gods might fly.
	But Oh! the people, brave and bold,
	from farm and forge and forest old--
	Rose up to break the tyrant's hand
	And cast him down to curse the land.
	They sealed his barrow with sacred flame,
	So none would chase his prideful claim.
	Let mortals walk the humble way,
	Lest night devour the dawn of day.

A secret unapproved verse:
	A mind too sharp for temple chains,
	Malel read the blood in rains.
	In ink long banned, he found the threads,
	Of gods now silent, voices fled.
	He begged them speak, he bled the land--
	A cursed crown in a trembling hand.
	They call him mad, and maybe so,
	But he saw the truth none else would know.
	A silence stirs beneath the stone,
	A king still waits beneath his throne.
	He burned for us, that we might see--
	What comes when gods no longer be.
