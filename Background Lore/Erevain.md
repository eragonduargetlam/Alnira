### **Erevain, the Kingdom Before**

Before **[[Malel]]'s** rise, **Erevain** was a broad and fertile realm — its glades and hills threaded by slow rivers and the smoke of shrine-fires. Each province kept its own stewards and seers, living by harvest and the favor of the divine. The people were bound more by faith than by crown or coin.

When **Malel** seized power, Erevain darkened. Temples closed or became war-rooms. Forbidden texts surfaced beneath torchlight. He bound the scattered lands beneath a single rule, bleeding their wealth into monuments and machines meant to stave off an unseen catastrophe.

Where once the gods were many, **silence** became the only truth worth fearing. Fortresses rose where sanctuaries once stood; new symbols overwrote the old. The land recoiled — rivers turned on their courses, birds flew without song, and storms learned to linger.

When Malel fell, the soil did not forgive. **Erevain** never truly healed. Old maps are ash in some towns, and certain hills still carry names whispered rather than spoken: **[[Malel’s Watch]]**, **[[The Withering Span]]**, **[[Barrowdeep]]**.

Yet the people endured. From ruin they rebuilt, reforged the faith, and in time raised their voices to the **[[New Gods]]**. To them, Erevain stands as both warning and lesson — a kingdom that sought to master the silence, and instead was consumed by it.

### **Sidebar Chronicle: “On the Echo of Erevain”**

_By Archscribe Tolen Veris, Keeper of the Dawn Archives, 412 A.N. (After the Silence)_

> “Every age builds its towers upon the bones of the last.  
> Erevain’s bones lie deep — and they shift still.”

Little survives of Erevain beyond fragments: cracked mosaics beneath newer chapels, roads that end in marshes, a few inscriptions whose meanings scholars still argue over. To some, Erevain was a cautionary tale — a warning of the dangers of unchecked rule and forbidden art. To others, it was a golden age whose fall was misinterpreted by fearful survivors.

The **New Gods’ clergy** preach that Erevain’s collapse was divine judgment: Malel’s defiance of silence invited the cleansing storm that reshaped the world. **Older folk traditions**, however, whisper that the land itself screamed before the gods ever stirred — that the silence Malel feared was not punishment, but prophecy.

Among the ruins, pilgrims still find signs that Erevain’s faith was not wholly lost. Burnt icons reveal faint halos of older runes beneath the newer symbols of the Concord. In the deeper barrows, some say you can still hear the rivers speaking in their sleep — murmuring the names of gods long forgotten.

Whether tyrant or savior, Malel’s shadow lingers. And though Varnhalde now rises upon the same soil, many who walk its fields still glance skyward when thunder gathers — and wonder if Erevain is stirring once more.
