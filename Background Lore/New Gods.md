Usurpers of the [[Hallows Above]], casting out the [[Old Gods]] in the process. Capable of speaking to those who reach out in true faith and able to witness the events of the near realms, [[Mortal Realm]] included.

They do have the ability to leave the [[Hallows Above]] and occupy other realms but they must sacrifice something of great personal importance to do so. It is rumored that these sacrifices have been the cause behind some of the great calamities to befall the [[Mortal Realm]]

### **The Four New Gods (The Tetrarchs / The Four Pillars)**

Each represents _power concentrated into a corner of existence_, ordered but inflexible. None are inherently good or evil — each can be worshiped as savior or cursed as tyrant.

1. **The Judge of Flame**    
    - **Domain:** Law, punishment, sacrifice, binding oaths.        
    - **Archetype:** The executioner in shining robes; the torch that burns both corruption and the innocent alike.        
    - **Ambiguity:** Brings order, but only through fire and pain.
        
2. **The Veiled Crown**    
    - **Domain:** Authority, fate, secrecy, hidden law.        
    - **Archetype:** The faceless ruler, masked and unknowable; whispers the “proper order” of the world.        
    - **Ambiguity:** Offers stability and vision — but demands obedience without question.
        
3. **The Iron Warden**    
    - **Domain:** Protection, strength, conquest, walls.        
    - **Archetype:** A fortress made flesh; defender of borders, but also the conqueror who builds them.        
    - **Ambiguity:** Shields the weak from chaos — but also cages them.
        
4. **The Silent Hallow**    
    - **Domain:** Death, silence, endings, the void.        
    - **Archetype:** A god who never speaks; their “word” is absence, their “gift” is the grave.  
    - **Ambiguity:** Offers peace, release, finality — but erases voice, history, and rebellion.

**Imagery & Symbolism**
- Four thrones set at the corners of a great square in the Hallows Above.
    
- Their symbols are angular, rigid, and geometric (squares, straight lines, four-pointed stars), in contrast to the **circles and crescents** of the Old Gods.
    
- Their priests call them _The Four Pillars_, claiming the heavens themselves would collapse without their weight.

### **Public-Facing Myth (Propaganda)**

- The Four are praised as the **“Pillars of the Dawn”**, who cast down the chaos of the Nine and built the ordered world mortals now enjoy.
    
- Priests say: _“The Nine bickered and squandered. The Four rose, united, and gave us law, fire, walls, and silence — the foundations of peace.”_
    
- Festivals celebrate their **stability**, often with squared layouts of altars, fourfold processions, and rituals of offering that always divide sacrifices into four parts.
    

---

### **Counter-Narrative (Whispered Truths)**

- Folk songs and old rhymes recall the Four as **“The Usurpers”**, beings who cut the world into corners and drained its color.
    
- The Old Gods’ disappearance is not seen as defeat but as an **ominous absence**: _“When the Nine sang, the heavens danced. When the Four rose, the heavens froze.”_
    
- Some secret sects mutter that the balance of **Nine Circles vs. Four Pillars** was never meant to hold — and now the collapse is coming.
