### 🕯️ Kingdom Name: Morvain

(Old Varnic: “Those Who Endure”)

- **Etymology:** **mor** = fate, **vain** = stone. Sturdy, cold, memorable.
    
- #### **Building Material — Osscrete**

Morvaini homes and civic structures are built primarily from **Osscrete** — a unique composite of lime, ash, powdered stone, and the finely ground remains of livestock and the honored dead.

- **Bone-lime binding** is considered sacred; it represents life reinforcing life. Every structure bears some fraction of ancestral remains, even if only ceremonial.
    
- For wealthier lineages, entire cornerstones or supporting beams may incorporate visible carved femurs or rib-struts — polished, lacquered, and inscribed with the names of those “Continuing” their duty in the structure.
    
- _“To dwell in Osscrete is to live within your family,”_ a common Morvani proverb, equivalent to “home is where the heart is” elsewhere.
    

#### **Cultural Contrast**

Morvani view timber architecture as both _wasteful_ and _foolish_:

- Wood burns, rots, and creaks. Bones, properly treated, endure centuries.
    
- Foreign embassies that insist on wooden beams are often mocked in quiet, polite tones — as if the ambassadors were children stacking kindling for the wind to knock over.
    
- Among masons, a popular saying:
    
    > _“The south builds with what feeds the fire; we build with what survives it.”_
    

#### **Typical Home Construction**

- **Foundation:** Deep stone footing mixed with ground ossuary ash for strength and symbolic blessing.
    
- **Walls:** Osscrete slabs poured into carved molds lined with ancestor sigils; reinforced internally with femur rods or sculpted vertebrae columns.
    
- **Roofing:** Heavy slate or overlapping bone plates treated with lye; the pale sheen is often mistaken for snow from a distance.
    
- **Windows & Light:** Minimal glass. Instead, they use thinly shaved bone or stretched treated hide for filtered light, producing a muted, perpetual dawn inside.
    
- **Decoration:** Relief carvings depicting family genealogies, spirals of bone dust set in mortar, and faint glows from phosphorescent lichens cultivated in small wall niches.
    

#### **Urban Aesthetic**

- Entire cities resemble solemn necropolises at first glance — angular, white-gray, and eerily uniform. Yet each building hums with familial identity, its inscriptions and embedded relics testifying to centuries of lineal contribution.
    
- When the wind blows across Morvani settlements, it catches the bone chimes strung on eaves — a sound like distant rain through hollow reeds.
    
- Fireplaces exist only in communal halls or for forge work. Domestic heat is produced by **soulstones**, alchemically treated mineral cores imbued with the gentle residual warmth of the recently departed.
    

#### **Religious and Practical Overlap**

Every home, regardless of wealth, includes a **Continuance Niche** — a recessed alcove containing the skulls or tokens of one’s ancestors.

- These serve both as memorial and conduit; Binders occasionally anoint them to awaken ancestral guidance in times of need.
    
- Foreign visitors often find this practice unnerving. Morvani find it barbaric to live _without_ their kin nearby.

### 🩶 In-World Social Note

The Dawnbreaker Synod frames Osscrete as “**the mortared sin**,” arguing that Morvain quite literally builds its cities atop the imprisoned dead.  
In Morvain, this accusation is met with weary laughter:

> _“Better bones in our walls than ashes on the wind.”_

---
    
- **Ethos:** Life is a cycle of usefulness—death does not end duty. Their necromancy isn’t an abomination but a craft of continuation. The undead are treated as _respected contributors_, not thralls. Families commission “Memory Binders” to ensure the soul’s consent and dignity before re-animation.
    
- **Visual Aesthetic:** Quiet cities of gray-white marble, veiled in incense smoke. Funereal colors (pale gold, ash, violet) dominate. Bells toll not in mourning, but as invitations for remembrance.
